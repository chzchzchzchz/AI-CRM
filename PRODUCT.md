# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

The everyday user is a B2B sales rep or account executive working a territory (the app is organized by region and company-size segment). They arrive with a job to be done, not time to browse: figure out which accounts are heating up, understand why, and take the next action — a call, an email, a follow-up — before a competitor does. Sales leaders and RevOps are a secondary audience who live in the analytics and pipeline views (buying-stage funnel, 6sense signals, forecast) to steer the team. When the two audiences conflict, the rep's in-the-moment workflow wins; the leadership views are read-oriented and can afford more density.

## Product Purpose

TargetDash is an AI prospect-intelligence layer that sits on top of a team's existing sales data — CRM/Salesforce accounts and contacts, 6sense intent, Gong calls, Clay enrichment — and turns it into a clear read on each account: what's happening, why it matters now, and what to do next. It exists because reps drown in raw signals and generic CRMs surface data without judgment. Success is a rep opening an account and, within seconds, trusting the brief enough to act on it — and the tool being good enough to sell as a standalone B2B product and to stand as portfolio-grade work.

## Positioning

The account brief every rep can trust: AI intelligence where every fact is computed from real data and every recommendation cites the signal behind it — grounded next-actions, never fabricated ones. That trust is the single claim every screen reinforces.

## Brand Personality

Sharp, modern, powerful. The voice is direct and expert — it tells the rep the thing, without hedging or filler. It should feel like a serious instrument built by people who understand sales, cutting-edge without being gimmicky. Confident and premium: the craft signals that the intelligence underneath is worth paying for. Restraint carries the confidence — it earns attention through precision and hierarchy, not through noise.

## Anti-references

Not the generic SaaS dashboard: the Bootstrap/Material sea of identical icon-heading-text cards, interchangeable and forgettable. Not Salesforce-style enterprise clutter: dense grey chrome, endless tabs, everything-configurable-and-nothing-clear legacy-CRM weight. Avoid data-everything overload (a wall of numbers with no point of view) and consumer-flashy gimmickry (gradients everywhere, emoji-heavy) — both undercut the trust the product is built on.

## Design Principles

Every number is real, and it shows its work. Facts are computed and presented with their evidence; the UI never implies precision it doesn't have, and gaps are stated plainly rather than filled with plausible-looking filler. This is the product's core credibility and its main differentiator.

Surface the signal, not the noise. Each screen leads with the one thing that matters — the next action, the account that's moving — and lets depth recede until asked for. Hierarchy does the work a wall of data can't.

Confident restraint. Premium comes from typography, spacing, and precision, not decoration. When in doubt, remove.

Built for daily speed. A rep lives here; every extra click, ambiguous label, or slow view is friction against the job. Fast reads, clear affordances, obvious next steps.

White-label by construction. The brand belongs to the deployer, so the visual system must be config-driven and strong-but-neutral — distinctive through structure and craft, not through a hardcoded identity.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA: body text at ≥4.5:1 contrast (a real risk given the dark, data-dense surfaces), visible focus states, labeled controls, and a `prefers-reduced-motion` alternative for every animation. Color-coded signals (intent heat, buying stage, sentiment) must never rely on color alone — pair with text or shape so they read for color-blind users.
